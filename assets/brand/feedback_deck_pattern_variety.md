In a single deck, **no pattern should appear more than twice** (rough cap). Stretch to three only when the pattern is the literal best fit and three is structurally inevitable. Reaching four uses of the same pattern means the deck reads as monotonous regardless of how good the individual slides are.

**Why:** On the May 2026 telecom corp redesign, I used P-06 capability-4 seven times in a 16-slide deck (44% of slides on one pattern). The result felt repetitive to David — even though each individual slide was content-correct. Pattern variety carries narrative rhythm. A reader scanning the deck should feel motion, not see the same 4-up grid recur.

**How to apply:**

When you find yourself reaching for P-06 (or any pattern) for a fourth time, stop. The content can almost always be expressed in another pattern, and the visual change is more valuable than the pattern-fit refinement:

- 4 items doesn't *have* to be P-06 capability-4. It can be:
  - P-08 module grid (2x2 within the 12-slot frame, with denser cells)
  - A custom "2x2 cap" variant (denser tiles than P-06, fits sub-points)
  - 2-column bullet list (a row per item with bold heading + description)
  - P-14 two-column comparison (if pairs naturally)
- 6 items can be:
  - P-07 capability-8 with 6 of 8 cells filled
  - P-08 module grid with 6 of 12 slots
  - 2-column bullet list (3 per column)
- 3 layers / 3 components can be P-12 radial knowledge graph or P-16 architecture diagram rather than another capability grid.
- A "what we deliver" slide doesn't need to be a grid at all — P-05 statement with sub-points underneath is a strong alternative.

The pattern-library lists ~16 patterns for a reason. A deck of 15–20 slides should exercise 8–12 of them, not 3.

**Audit step before drafting HTML:** After mapping every slide to a pattern in the outline, count instances. If any pattern is over 2, reassign at least one to the next-best alternative. Treat the Metafore deck like a magazine layout — every spread looks different.
